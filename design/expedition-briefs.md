# The twenty-four expeditions

First-person C/SDL3 campaign, solo or two seats. Each portal can be a player's first. Arrival teaches movement, aim, Use and Lift again in its own vocabulary. A December keepsake restores one place at the hall table. Plan for a twenty-minute first visit across five named sectors.

The design below sets the purpose before the maps. The accompanying [walkthrough and measured plans](route-audit.md) trace these ideas into the simulation. Each row gives the decisions in play order. A roadside action refills lift and gives a short travelling boost. The side-room keepsake protects the party from one sentry contact and opens a return passage from inside. Both players share the lock. The optional piece is distinct from the keepsake that goes back to the hall.

## Rhythm and threats

Read each mission's five sector names in order as **teach (2), develop (5), twist (7), recover and recombine (4), shelter (2), final three-part demonstration (9), extraction (2)**. Shelter is inside the fifth sector. At the first gate players see the side-room beacon before earning its return line. The starting lesson and shelter have no contact hazard. Gold points to the compulsory objective. Mint marks optional field work and the return station. A pulsing marker is ready on its bright beat. Walking past a field mark is safe.

The roaming paper sentry (threat 2/5) patrols the active tool. Contact at ground level returns the player to a nearby checkpoint. Aimed fire stuns it, lift clears it, and a recovered side-room ward absorbs one contact. The moving shutters (2/5), timing lenses (2/5), and paired light orbs (3/5) ask for aim or timing and permit unlimited retries. Raised rings (2/5) and low gates (1/5) have a walkable floor beneath them. Each level renames these actors in its tool list and encounter roster. A missed signal costs an opportunity for speed. Optional pieces persist until the next sector. Live tickets and rehearsal checkpoints remain separate.
